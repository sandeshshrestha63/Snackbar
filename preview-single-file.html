About the kitchen

A chulo is the clay hearth every Nepali home was built around. We named the restaurant after it, and then we cooked on one.

The menu is short and it changes with what the market has. We make our own pickles, our own curd and our own gundruk, and we do not hide the kitchen from you.

Our story

Started at
a hearth.

A small restaurant in Bakulahar with a big opinion about charcoal.

आगो

Growing up around Chitwan, the best meals were never in restaurants. They were at somebody's aunt's house, on a clay chulo, with a pot that had been used for thirty years and a cook who never measured anything.

We wanted to bring that table to the road. Not a museum version of Nepali food, and not a fusion menu with a Nepali name stuck on it. Just the dishes we grew up with, cooked with a bit more care and served with a bit more light.

That is why the kitchen is built around a real wood and charcoal hearth. It is slower, hotter and harder to control than gas. It is also the only way to get the skin on the chulo chicken right, and the only way to get the smoke into the sekuwa.

We buy from farms and markets close by wherever we can. The curd comes from a neighbour. The greens come from the field behind the kitchen when the season allows. When something is not good that week, it comes off the menu.

House rules

Three things we
will not change

  1. Fire, not shortcuts

    If a dish is meant to be cooked over coals, it is cooked over coals. No smoke flavouring, no tricks.

  2. Make it ourselves

    Pickles, curd, gundruk, spice blends. Anything we can make in the kitchen, we do.

  3. Feed people properly

    Dal refills are free. Portions are generous. Nobody leaves hungry, and nobody gets rushed.

Good food is mostly time, a hot fire, and someone who cares whether you finish your plate.
